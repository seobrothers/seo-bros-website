We spend most of our time running local SEO for other people's clients. Earlier this year we decided to run a proper experiment on ourselves: publish city-level research pages at scale, measure everything, and let the data decide what happens next.

Between February and early July we shipped 55 of them, one per city, each targeting "[city] seo" with real keyword data for that market. Four were older hand-built originals. The rest went out in waves through June and early July.

The rule we set going in: no second wave until the first one proves something. Measure first, then decide. It is the same discipline we push on agencies who want fifty location pages for a client by Friday, so it seemed only fair to eat our own cooking.

Ten weeks of Search Console later, here is what the experiment has actually taught us.

## What ten weeks of Search Console shows

Two data pulls, overlapping windows. The first covered June 1 through July 19. The second covered July 6 through August 12.

| Window | Pages with impressions | Total impressions | Clicks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jun 1 to Jul 19 | 35 of 55 | 1,832 | 3 |
| Jul 6 to Aug 12 | 45 of 55 | 2,468 | 1 |

Before anyone panics about the click column: we knew the volume math going in. "[city] seo" terms run small, and "white label seo [city]" is close to zero everywhere, which is why these pages were built as research assets and entity signals rather than conversion pages. The conversion terms live elsewhere on the site. The question this experiment asks is narrower: will Google surface programmatically planned but hand-written city pages at all, and which ones earn a position?

The answer so far is a clear "most of them, slowly." Forty-five of 55 pages now register impressions, up from 35 a few weeks earlier. Several sit in real positions on small samples: Denver at 5.4, Tucson at 5.0, Toronto at 7.1, Birmingham at 5.8 with the one click. New pages on a five-year-old domain took four to six weeks to start registering, and the ramp is still climbing.

That is the aggregate. The interesting findings are in the pages that broke the pattern.

## Thin pages earned nothing

Ten pages have zero impressions after two months. When we lined that list up against the content, the overlap was hard to argue with: almost all of them came from one batch that shipped at 800 to 850 words, while the pages earning impressions average 1,150 to 1,300.

Same template, same structure, same kind of keyword data. The difference is depth: the thin batch stops after the volume chart, while the deeper pages explain how the metro actually behaves, which sub-markets carry their own demand, and what that means for a business picking its battles.

We had a hunch length was not the real variable so much as substance, but either way the market has voted. Google is apparently willing to index and rank a hand-written city page with real data in it, and apparently unwilling to bother with the 800-word version of the same idea.

This week we rebuilt all twelve of the thinnest pages to the standard of the ones that rank, with fresh keyword data pulled for each market. If the zero-impression set starts registering over the next month, that is the cleanest content-depth result we have ever produced on our own dime.

## Presence beats prose

One page produced 1,780 of the 2,468 impressions in the last window. That is 72 percent of the entire experiment coming from a single city: Halifax.

The Halifax page is not our best page. It is one of the four originals, and several newer pages are better researched. What Halifax has that Denver does not is us. SEO Brothers is a Nova Scotia company, headquartered in Halifax, with a Google Business Profile that links to the site.

That one entity connection outweighs everything we did editorially across the other 54 cities. It is the local SEO lesson in miniature: content earns you a seat at the table, but presence signals decide who Google actually trusts in a market. We tell clients this constantly. Watching it happen on our own property, with a 72-to-28 split, was still striking.

The corollary matters for anyone building location pages: a page for a city where the business has no physical presence, no profile, and no reviews is playing a fundamentally different game than the page for the home market. Plan for that difference instead of being surprised by it.

## Impressions come before clicks, by a lot

The pages sitting at positions 5 through 8 are earning impressions and almost no clicks. That is normal and worth saying out loud, because this is the stage where most people conclude the content failed.

Bottom of page one on a low-volume commercial term is a visibility position, not a traffic position. The click curve on these terms is brutally top-heavy, and small-sample average positions bounce around. The work at this stage is boring: deepen what is thin, keep internal links flowing (we shipped a region-based interlinking mesh across all 55 pages in late July, and the number of pages earning impressions jumped from 35 to 45 in the weeks that followed, though we cannot fully separate that from ordinary ramp time), and re-measure on a schedule instead of reacting to every wiggle.

Our next checkpoint is mid-September. If the deepened pages start registering and the climbers keep climbing, the data says widen to the tier-one metros we deliberately skipped, where the volumes are real: "los angeles seo" at 1,800 searches a month, "boston seo" at 1,400, "miami seo" at 1,300, most of them at single-digit or low-double-digit difficulty. If not, we will have learned that too, in public, for the price of some writing.
