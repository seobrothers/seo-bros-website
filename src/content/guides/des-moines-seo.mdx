Des Moines is one of the most concentrated markets on this list. Demand lands almost entirely on the metro term, the suburbs return little or nothing as standalone searches, and even the larger ring communities, West Des Moines, Ankeny, function as part of the Des Moines search rather than markets of their own.

The shape makes sense once you look at how the metro is built. Des Moines is Iowa's capital, its largest city by a wide margin, and the anchor of the state's insurance industry, with no second city in the region pulling identity away from it. The suburbs, even fast-growing ones like Ankeny and Waukee, are bedroom communities of one dominant core, and nothing out there is more than about twenty minutes from downtown. One center plus short distances means residents reach for the metro term or "near me," and standalone suburb searches never develop.

There is also a phrasing quirk worth knowing: in this market "roofing Des Moines" pulls far more than "roofers Des Moines," which changes how you target the pages.

The observations here come from running local campaigns in markets shaped like this one. We use roofing as the sample service below because demand for it is broad and storm-sensitive, and central Iowa sits in a hail-prone belt, which makes the cross-area variation a clean read on search behavior. The same shape holds for HVAC and plumbing at different absolute numbers.

## What "Des Moines" means in search

**Des Moines as the city** covers downtown, the close neighborhoods, and the inner suburbs that feel like Des Moines. **Greater Des Moines or the DSM metro** stretches across Polk County and into Dallas and Warren counties, taking in the fast-growing western suburbs. **Central Iowa** is the regional frame.

A keyword tool reports "Des Moines roofing" as one line, and in this market that line genuinely carries most of the metro's demand.

## The markets that actually carry volume

Des Moines itself carries almost all of it. **Ankeny**, the fast-growing northern suburb, registers modestly. **West Des Moines**, **Waukee**, **Urbandale**, and the college town of **Ames** to the north return little standalone search despite being real, growing places, their residents search the metro term or "near me."

- **Des Moines**, **DSM**: the dominant core term.
- **Ankeny**: northern suburb, modest standalone search.
- **West Des Moines**, **Waukee**, **Urbandale**: western suburbs that roll up into the metro.
- **Ames**: college town to the north, its own small market but light commercial search.

Two findings here. First, concentration: Des Moines carries the demand and the suburbs barely register, so the suburb pages most contractors build chase volume that isn't there. Second, phrasing: "roofing Des Moines" runs about 500 a month and "Des Moines roofing" another 200, far more than "roofers Des Moines." The pattern holds on other trades. Sampled fresh in August 2026 (Ahrefs, US targeting): "plumbers des moines" runs about 400 a month with "plumber des moines" adding 250, "hvac des moines" runs 350, "electrician des moines" 200, and the repair terms are strong, with "ac repair des moines" and "furnace repair des moines" at about 250 each. And the phrasing quirk repeats: the plural "plumbers des moines" beats the singular by a wide margin, just as "roofing" beats "roofers." This market has its own vocabulary.

## What this means for local SEO in Des Moines

**Win the metro term and the map pack first.** Demand concentrates on "Des Moines roofing / [service]" and "[service] near me," and because the suburbs contribute so little standalone search, the Des Moines term is effectively the whole market. Profile and reviews lead, backed by a genuinely strong metro service page.

**Match the local phrasing.** Here "roofing Des Moines" beats "roofers Des Moines" and "plumbers des moines" beats "plumber des moines." Target the phrasing the market actually uses, because in a single-term market the phrasing decision is a large share of the strategy.

**Don't over-build suburb pages.** West Des Moines, Waukee, Urbandale, and Ames return little standalone volume across every trade we sampled. Ankeny is the one suburb that earns consideration, at 30 to 90 monthly searches depending on the service, and even that is a judgment call. Build only for areas that generate real demand.

**Hold repair pages year round for the storm cycle.** Central Iowa's hail belt means roofing demand spikes after storms, and the steady 250 a month each on "ac repair des moines" and "furnace repair des moines" shows repair intent runs all year. A repair page that already ranks when the hail hits captures the surge; one built afterward misses it.

## Common questions about SEO in Des Moines

### Should I build a page for West Des Moines or Waukee?

The data says no, not as ranking plays. "Roofers west des moines" and "roofers waukee" each return about 10 monthly searches, and the pattern repeats on other trades. These are real, growing places, but their residents search "Des Moines" or "near me." A location page for an office you actually have is fine; a templated set of suburb ranking pages is wasted effort.

### Is Ankeny worth its own page?

It's the borderline case. Ankeny is the one suburb that consistently registers, roughly 30 monthly searches on the roofing sample and up to 90 on "plumber ankeny." A business based in Ankeny or heavily serving it can justify a locally specific page. For everyone else, the metro page covers it.

### Why does "roofing Des Moines" beat "roofers Des Moines"?

Markets develop their own phrasing habits, and Des Moines leans toward the service noun over the trade noun: "roofing Des Moines" at about 500 a month against 150 for "roofers des moines." The same lean shows up in plumbing, where "plumbers des moines" (400) beats the singular (250). Your page titles and headings should match what people type, and the national-default phrasing would leave most of this market's volume on the table.

### How competitive is Des Moines compared to bigger metros?

The volumes are smaller than a coastal metro, but concentration cuts both ways. In the August 2026 Ahrefs pull, "hvac des moines" scores a difficulty of 70, high for a market this size, because every competitor fights over the same single term. The map pack matters more here for the same reason: three spots serve essentially the whole metro.
