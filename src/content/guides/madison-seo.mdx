Madison is a compact, concentrated market. As a state capital and university town, its commercial search lands hard on the city name, the close suburbs barely register as standalone searches, and the one genuine separate market is Janesville, far enough south to behave on its own. For a service business, that means the strategy is the metro term plus a real Janesville presence, not a spread of suburb pages.

Geography explains a lot of it. Downtown Madison sits on an isthmus between Lake Mendota and Lake Monona, with the Capitol and the University of Wisconsin sharing the strip. The city grew outward from that squeeze, and the suburbs, Middleton, Fitchburg, Verona, Sun Prairie, grew as extensions of it rather than as towns with separate commercial lives. Verona hosts Epic Systems, one of the region's biggest employers, and even that hasn't turned Verona into a place people search for a plumber by name.

Janesville is different. It sits about forty miles south in Rock County, a manufacturing town with its own history and its own orbit, and its residents search their own city's name.

The observations here come from running local campaigns in markets shaped like this one. We use HVAC as the sample service below because it exists wherever there are houses, which makes the cross-area variation a read on search behavior rather than something specific to one trade.

## What "Madison" means in search

**Madison as the city** covers the isthmus, the near west and east sides, and the close neighborhoods. **Greater Madison or Dane County** stretches to the suburban ring and the smaller communities around the lakes. **South-central Wisconsin** is the regional frame for businesses that reach toward Janesville and Beloit.

A keyword tool reports "Madison HVAC" as one line, but the searcher might mean the city, the county, or the wider region.

## The markets that actually carry volume

The genuinely searched entity is Madison itself, with **Janesville** as a real second market about forty miles south. The close suburbs, Sun Prairie, Fitchburg, Middleton, Verona, are growing fast, Sun Prairie has been one of the fastest-growing cities in Wisconsin, but their residents still search the metro term or "near me," not the suburb plus a service.

- **Madison**: the core term.
- **Janesville**: a separate market to the south, its own commercial search.
- **Sun Prairie**, **Fitchburg**, **Middleton**, **Verona**: close suburbs that roll up into Madison.
- **Dane County**, **south-central Wisconsin**: regional overlays for service-area research.

The shape is the finding. Madison carries the demand, Janesville is a genuine second target, and the close suburbs return little as standalone searches. The suburb pages most contractors build for Middleton and Verona chase volume that isn't there; the leverage is the metro term, the map pack, and a real Janesville page for businesses that reach that far south.

The pattern holds on other trades, and the concentration gets even sharper. Fresh numbers from August 2026 (Ahrefs, US targeting): "roof repair madison wi" runs 300 a month and "roofers madison wi" 150, with "plumbers madison wi" at 150 and "electrician madison wi" at 70. Meanwhile "roofers janesville wi" returns just 20, "plumber janesville wi" effectively nothing, and "plumber sun prairie wi" shows 10. Janesville's standalone demand is real for HVAC, but it thins out fast on other trades, so weigh that page by the trade you're in. The suburbs stay near zero across the board.

## What this means for local SEO in Madison

**Win the metro term and the map pack first.** Demand concentrates on "Madison [service]" and "[service] near me." In a one-term market, the local pack for that term is most of the game, so profile quality, review velocity, and a strong core service page matter more here than anywhere.

**Treat Janesville as its own market, but check your trade first.** At 100 monthly HVAC searches, a real Janesville page earns its keep for a heating and cooling company. At 20 for "roofers janesville wi," a roofer weighs it differently. If your trucks already run down the Interstate, the page costs little; just build it on real demand, not habit.

**Don't over-build suburb pages.** Sun Prairie, Fitchburg, Middleton, and Verona return little standalone volume, and the August pull confirms it across trades. A growing population is not the same thing as standalone search. These residents orient to Madison and type accordingly.

**The barrier to entry is lower than the big metros.** In the August 2026 pull, Ahrefs scores "roof repair madison wi" at just 9 difficulty and "roofers madison wi" at 24, with the plumbing and electrical terms in the 30s. Those are modest numbers by metro standards, which means a focused campaign moves faster here than the same effort would in Milwaukee or Chicago.

## Common questions about SEO in Madison

### Should I build pages for Sun Prairie, Middleton, Fitchburg, or Verona?

The data says no. Every suburb query we've sampled comes back at or near zero, "plumber sun prairie wi" managed 10 a month in August 2026, and that's the strongest of the set. Residents of these towns search "Madison [service]" or "[service] near me" and let proximity sort it out. The exception is physical presence: an office in Middleton naturally wins west-side map pack results, and the Madison page does the organic work.

### Is a Janesville page actually worth it?

For HVAC, yes: 100 monthly searches is a genuine second market, and it's the biggest standalone number in the region outside Madison itself. For roofing and plumbing, the August numbers are thin, 20 and effectively zero. The honest answer is trade-dependent, so pull the number for your own service before you commit.

### Why do Madison's search volumes look so small for a metro this size?

Because the demand doesn't fragment. There are no named submarkets splitting the total the way Durham and Chapel Hill split the Triangle, and a large share of local intent flows through "near me" queries that never show up on a city-term line. The keyword tool's number understates the market; the map pack sees all of it. Low chart numbers here mean concentration, not absence.

### Can one company rank in both Madison and Janesville?

Organically, yes, with a genuinely local page for each. The map pack is harder: the cities are forty miles apart and Google's proximity weighting means a Madison address rarely surfaces in Janesville pack results. Businesses serious about both markets either establish a real presence in each or accept that Janesville will be an organic-only play.
