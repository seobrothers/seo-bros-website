The brand was strong on the ground but invisible on Google. Seven organic sessions a month, no top-100 rankings for the terms that drive rentals, and a service area spread across small Western Colorado communities where buyers search with high local specificity.

In sparse regional markets, that's good news. The competitive density is low, the search behavior is predictable, and the barrier is execution rather than budget.

## Audit findings

[Specific cities and competitor details redacted at the partner's request.]

The site was a single-template build with no dedicated pages for the communities it actually served, and no separation between roll-off, dumpster, and portable sanitation as distinct service lines. Three buyer intents collapsed onto one landing page.

## What we shipped

Month one was foundation work: technical fixes, an information architecture rebuild, and a clean service taxonomy that separated the three product lines.

Months two and three were coverage. We built dedicated location pages for each community in the service area and category pages for each service line, with internal links pointing equity at the money pages.

Months four and five were where the compounding showed up. Once the new pages were indexed and the link graph was settled, rankings jumped fast.

## Outcome

Traffic moved from 7 monthly organic sessions to 202, a 2,786% lift over five months. The improvements concentrated where they earn revenue: top-three placement for "rent a porta potty" and "portable toilets" terms across the priority cities, with double-digit gains on the harder competitive terms.

Tracked GA4 leads climbed from zero in month one to 25 by the end of month five, and the growth curve was still climbing when the campaign window closed.

In a regional service market with five competitors, the team that ships first wins for years. The window to establish search authority in categories like this is shorter than most operators realize.
