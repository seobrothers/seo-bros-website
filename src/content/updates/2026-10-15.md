A day spent getting things to land where they belong: report numbers on the right part of a site, approval emails in the right inbox, and a discovery that can start without anyone shepherding it.

## For our partners

1. Reports for a franchise location measure that location's section of the site rather than the whole brand domain.
2. When a client's contacts are set to receive plan approvals or draft approvals, they get a review email with their own share link as soon as the approval opens.
3. A discovery link can be handed out to collect a prospect's website and send the finished report back by email.

### 1. Location scope on franchise reports

A location that lives in a folder on a brand site was compared against local competitors with the brand's numbers standing in for it, which told a partner nothing about the location. Traffic, keywords, backlinks and top pages now read the location's section of the site. Domain rating stays at the domain level, because that is a property of the domain and not of a folder. Where the location's main page sits outside the folder, it is folded in only when it is a page in its own right, so the same links are not counted twice. Existing discoveries pick this up on refresh.

### 2. Approvals that reach the client

When the Contacts page says a client's contacts receive a type of approval, every approval of that type that opens sends them a review email with their share link, including approvals an agent opens on its own and the reminder before an auto-approval. The Approvals switches in the Add contact dialog now set the same thing the Delivery tab reads, so turning a type on takes effect where you turned it on. The client email pause still holds everything. Approvals already open are unchanged.

### 3. Discovery quick links

The form carries the partner's branding, and the website plus a destination email are the only required fields; anything else is context. The report comes back to that address with both the client-facing page and the workspace link, and the proposal section can be left off for a cold prospect. A link can be given an expiry and a cap on uses, and can be turned off at any time.

## Behind the scenes

Our agents could only ever run on a campaign or a website. They now run on a record of any kind: a call, an email thread, a task, an escalation, a note, or our own business with no client behind it at all. The useful part was deciding where that fact lives. What a run is about belongs to the run, stamped when it starts, not to a permission sitting on the agent, and we walked that back the same day we first built it the other way. Before any of it moved, we replayed a month of the schedule against the live one, slot for slot, and required zero differences. Reviewers also got a note box on every task and plan: rather than write a checklist for what good looks like, we would sooner read what they actually say and find the themes.

Agents that work in a terminal share one machine, and the schedule handed it everything due at the same moment, because nothing in the design let the machine refuse. Three things changed. A run now closes its session the moment it finishes instead of sitting there; the machine accepts a set number of runs at once and turns the rest away, and turned-away work comes back a few minutes later; and the count includes runs still starting up, which do not look like runs yet. The thing doing the work needs a say in how much of it arrives.
