Most of today's work was about timing: when a due date should start counting, when a website read should land, and what a listing page is really telling us. Small moves, but they each remove a wrong answer a partner would otherwise have to explain.

## For our partners

1. A task that was waiting on a decision is now due tomorrow when it is approved, instead of landing in the team's queue already past due.
2. Setup reads your website while the crawl runs, so the Locations screen opens with the right map and the right questions for the kind of business you have.
3. Directory listings that print the directory's own phone number now read as not applicable, with a note explaining why.
4. An audit belongs to a website rather than to a campaign, so a client can buy one without an ongoing campaign.
5. A workspace with no brands yet gets a New brand button on the Clients page.

### 1. Due dates start when the work does

When an approval takes a few days, the work only becomes available on the day it is approved. Until now the due date had been counting the whole time, so the task arrived red. An approved task with a date of today or earlier now moves to tomorrow at 5pm Eastern. A date still in the future stays where it was, and the planned month never moves.

### 2. Locations knows what kind of business you are

The read of your website now starts at the same moment as the crawl rather than after it, which means it arrives in time to shape the screens that follow. For a business customers travel to, the Locations map opens closer in, the service-area trace is optional, and the questions ask which neighbourhoods and towns your customers come from and where you want more. Service-area businesses keep the questions that suit them. If the read is still coming, the screen says so rather than guessing.

### 3. A directory's own phone number is not a mismatch

Some directories print their own head office or support line where the practice's phone would go. Every one of those read as a wrong number and sat in the correction queue with nothing to correct. Those now read as not applicable, and hovering explains why, either that the number belongs to the site or that the site never shows that field. Any other number on those sites, and those numbers anywhere else, are judged as before.

### 4. Audits are a website's job

An audit is now created against a website, not a campaign. The order form, the audit pages and the delivered report all key on the website and pull location profiles from the client's own locations.

## Behind the scenes

Two structural moves landed, both of the kind where the first half was done weeks ago and the second half was still standing.

The first: a campaign's scope now hangs off the service it belongs to rather than the campaign as a whole. That sounds like bookkeeping, but it was producing wrong answers. A client with an ongoing campaign and a separate one-time project had the project's work counted toward the monthly promise. Writing scope onto a client with no service line yet now creates the line first, so there is no window where the work exists and nothing owns it. We compared every campaign's old reading against the new one before shipping: they agree everywhere except the handful we knew were wrong, which are now right.

The second: approvals now record the due-date move they caused. A date that changes with no trace of why is a date nobody trusts.

We also moved the help articles out of a database and into the same place the product's own definitions live, so an article can ride along with the change it explains. The build refuses an article that names the product the wrong way, or that uses our internal word for something where a partner would use theirs, and it marks a video stale once the article's body has moved on from what the video shows. Documentation that cannot drift is worth more than documentation that is merely thorough.

On the delivery side, the team's queue stopped using "blocked" as a parking spot for work waiting on an approval. Waiting on a decision is now its own state. Blocked means blocked, which makes the blocked list worth reading again.
