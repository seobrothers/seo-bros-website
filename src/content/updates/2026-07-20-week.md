The theme this week was the client side: where the client portal can live, and how approving and reviewing work feels.

## For our partners

The whole client portal can now be embedded anywhere as one no-login page: overview and approvals, keyword mapping, plans, tasks, activity and reports, reached by a private per-client link the partner manages from the client's Client Portal tab. Approvals and feedback work there exactly as they do for a logged-in client, with an optional email check for visitors. The same portal is available inside HighLevel: agencies connect their HighLevel account, map sub-accounts to clients, and clients see their campaign (performance, plans, work feed, approvals, and a way to request a discovery) inside the CRM they already use.

Reviewing work got one shape. Approve, request changes and decline are explicit choices. Feedback is a batch: highlight-to-comment on the piece, a notes list, one send-back. The decision lives in the sidebar, and approved content locks. The approvals queue became a filterable table, grouped by section, with type and client filters on the all-clients view. Google Business post items open as a full post: image, words, link and schedule.

The portal home became Overview, with a campaign intro the partner writes in their own words, and approvals got their own page. Activity became the client's work log, written in the partner's voice ("your strategist", never a robot), with plan approvals reading as alignment. Share pages wear the partner's sidebar accent. Competitors moved onto the client's Setup page, and campaign scope got a Configuration tab where each line's client-facing wording can be overridden. Discoveries and audits moved onto the shared data table, and team assignment on a client became a multi-select.

## Behind the scenes

Which AI model an agent uses is chosen by our team, set per kind of task with a platform default behind it, so partners never pick or see one. Citations resolve a business's category from its own data. A platform-wide pause for rank alerts sits behind a staff switch for days when a data provider misbehaves.

Staff can become the strategist for a campaign from the API: pull the same briefing the agent would get and work it by hand. Website tools joined the rest of the staff API, and the AI tools on a staff member's own desktop can work the platform as them. The scripts that move our book of agencies onto the platform learned its quirks: a live guest post lands as a closed link item, placeholder tasks are skipped, and everything brought across is attributed to our team rather than the partner's.
