The week the platform stopped being a prototype. Real billing switched on Monday, and the agent system was rebuilt so that every agent is something staff can see and edit rather than something written into code.

## For our partners

Live billing switched on July 27. The platform also got a new mark, The Swell, a wave waterline, across the app.

Screenshots became evidence. Every capture the agent takes is kept and can be pinned to a task, at capture time or later, and the client portal shows them on the task. Partners and staff can add and remove them too. Google Business post items carry their published truth: the live Google link, or the moderation rejection if there was one. Website map became Keyword Mapping everywhere a client sees it.

For agencies we manage under our own brand, a new shape: instead of creating tasks, they request them. A request lands with our team, who promote it to a task or close it with a note the requester sees. Those partners default to quiet email and to our team implementing website changes, and anything staff can see that the partner cannot wears an obvious amber Staff pill. Partners can also message the agent directly from a steering lane on the requests table, and the messages are kept as history.

Approved content is final: no new versions and no re-asks. Task decisions reach the agent for team and provider tasks, and the task page shows the approval history and notes thread. Page monitoring shows checks rather than grades; the score gauge is gone. Team admins can change a member's role after the fact, and members can be granted edit access to the agent's guidance. Discovery keyword tables tag each row with its market.

## Behind the scenes

Agent Builder v2 landed between Thursday and Saturday. Every agent is now a row with an identity, the documents it reads, the data sets it is handed, a trigger (a schedule, an event such as a matched meeting, or a task becoming ready), a visibility setting and a history. Context is assembled from per-agent data sets rather than a fixed profile. Schedules follow a recipe grammar with per-campaign overrides, and campaigns review daily while onboarding, then settle into weekly, monthly or twice-monthly rhythms. By Sunday no agent lived in code: a run that resolves no agent row does nothing rather than falling back to a hardcoded strategist, and staff can become any agent by name.

Staff can chat with a finished run: fork its session, ask what it was thinking, and optionally let it act, with a different model for a second opinion. The agent learned to read the local map picture for a competitive review. Approval requests work for every task kind, not only content. Campaign requests notify the assigned staff, and no assigned staff raises an ops alert. Failures in profiling a new client, or in the crawler's account, raise an alert too.

We also wrote down the Copy Gate: four tests every new sentence of interface copy must pass before it ships, the default being silence. It is the reason the app says less than you might expect.
