Most of today went into Listings: who a page actually belongs to, how accurate a location is, and finishing the work a listing asks of you. The rest was dates in the client portal and a directory we could see but could not read.

## For our partners

1. Listings tells a practice's own pages apart from its doctors' profiles, scores each location's accuracy out of 100, and lets you finish a hand-off on the page it sits on.
2. The counts above the Listings table are one row instead of three sets that disagreed with each other, and pressing a count filters the table underneath it.
3. Client plan pages in the portal have a Published column of their own, and upcoming site edits show the day they are scheduled for.
4. Listings can read Foursquare once your own Places API key is connected, under Settings > Integrations & Setup > Listings.
5. A client activated with a future start date reads as scheduled on the roster and in the client header, instead of as a prospect. Thank you to the partner who asked whether that activation had taken; it had, and now the screen says so.
6. In the connector, the campaign brief tools are now campaign goal tools, and you can hand a client request straight to the agent that takes in and triages requests.

### 1. Practice pages, doctor pages, and a number for accuracy

On a client with several practitioners, the practice's own pages and each doctor's profile sat in one list, so neither count meant much. Every listing is now classed as the practice's page, a practitioner's profile or a search page, and the table carries a Practice and Doctors switch with coverage per doctor.

The accuracy score is weighted rather than a flat percentage: the sites that move the needle count for more, doctor profiles count for less, and the header says the day the listings were last read. There is a trend behind it and an overview across your clients with the worst locations first. The pages read in your agency's brand throughout.

A hand-off is now one card: the need in a line, who does what, and three ways to close it, done, can't do it, or ask us. What you write on the last two comes back to us as work waiting on our side.

### 4. Foursquare with your own key

A Foursquare link could be attached but never read, so the listing stayed empty of detail and corrections there never settled. It is now read like any other directory, with one difference: the key is yours, created in your own Foursquare developer account, because the listings, the account and its free monthly allowance belong to you.

Once the key is connected, rechecks follow a correction instead of passing over it, and a discovery audit searches Foursquare near the business and files the match along with any duplicates it finds. Without a key, Foursquare behaves as it does today.

## Behind the scenes

A piece of content can now be read two ways: what was approved, and what may ship. The second refuses when the newest draft differs from the text the approval was stamped on, so an edit saved after the approval cannot go live under it. An identical re-save passes, because nothing changed. Every publishing step, including the ones our agents run, reads the second one.

In the staff queue, work that is stuck now sits with its own kind of work rather than in one pile, and a task that failed leads with what failed and when, with links to the agent runs behind it. Reading a failure used to mean guessing which run produced it.

The API and the connector tools also come from a single list now, and the written reference is generated from that list rather than kept by hand, so the two cannot drift apart.
