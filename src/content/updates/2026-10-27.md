Almost all of today went into the website side of the platform: what an agent can see when it opens a site, what it can change safely, and how a site gets started in the first place.

## For our partners

1. A text search now reaches every page of a site: a phrase, leftover brackets from a paste, odd spacing or links, with the entry and the field each hit sits in. You can replace only the hits you pick.
2. New tools for bringing an old site over: screenshots at laptop and phone width, a page read section by section, the redirect list, and a check of the old site's addresses against the new one.
3. Sites can be listed, created and set up without opening the wizard, on the theme and the domain you name.
4. A form can take a file upload and the lead keeps the file, a page can sit under a parent, and a page marked noindex stays out of the sitemap.
5. Reply to a notification email from the platform and it reaches our support desk.

### 1. Search and replace across a site

The search covers what is published on the preview site: entries, sections, rich text, and the fixed labels a theme prints ("Call or text", "Hours", a form's "Name"). Replacing one of those labels rewords it everywhere the theme uses it.

Links can be added on an anchor phrase, with the guards in the tool rather than in a reviewer's head: the target has to be a page of the same site, never the page it sits on, never inside an existing link, and never a second link to the same target. The same screen carries the list of addresses people asked for and did not find, and alt text on library images, with the missing ones marked.

### 2. Bringing an old site over

An agent can now look at what it is matching: the list of pages the preview site renders, a page read section by section, and screenshots of any page or URL at two widths. Redirects are a list you can read and add to, and they carry to the live site when it publishes. The old-URL check compares the last crawl of the old site against the new one and tells you what has nowhere to land. It writes nothing.

### 3. Starting a site without the wizard

Listing and creating sites, the list of themes, and a single setup call that does what the wizard's launch step does. Create takes the theme you want (the starter theme is now the default) and the domain, matched to one of the client's websites, so a client with several sites gets one on the right one.

A site created this way builds on the theme and stops: nothing is read from the old site, nothing is filled in. Its page shows the cards, the domain and the connector tabs, without the onboarding steps that only describe the automated build.

## Behind the scenes

Our agents work inside whatever builder a client already has, and today one met a builder we had not worked in before. Two pages were taken through it and the playbook written from what they found: where the page settings live, what "hide this page from search" actually writes, how to verify past the cache, and the trap worth the most, that publish pushes the whole site with no confirmation, so you check the last publish against the last save before you press it. A second pass covered images: what the uploader accepts, the overlay that resets on every background swap, and what a page does for an hour after a publish drops an image.

We also rebuilt how we track our own work. Small fixes have their own queue instead of being written up as projects, everything else sits on one board, and a ticket is a single page: what came in, what it is for, the steps on it and the conversation. The habit is the point. A thing is either a few minutes of work or it is on the board.
