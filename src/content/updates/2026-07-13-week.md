The product got its name this week, the client portal became a set of real pages, reports learned to chart, and the first scripts for moving our existing agency book off the old tools were written.

## For our partners

The platform became Tideworthy on Monday: new name across the app, a new mark, a canonical address, and email that sends from a Tideworthy account domain. Partners send client email from a subdomain of their own domain, managed for them, instead of bringing their own email keys. Branding settings split into Client Portal and Email Sending tabs, and a partner's own logo replaces ours at the top of their sidebar. A shield in the top bar holds two account-wide trust controls, pause all client communication and force safe mode across every campaign, with per-client safe mode underneath.

The client portal went from one plan page to a set of pages: Content, Link building, Google Business posts, Website map, Activity, Approvals and Reports. Each page shows only when the partner turns it on and there is something to show. Clients approve plans and content from the portal itself, not only from an emailed link, and pending decisions lead their dashboard. Client comments with mentions arrived, with an inbox on the client workspace.

Reporting grew charts: six-month monthly trends with the year prior overlaid, stat cards with sparklines, a crawl score gauge, crawl history as a stacked bar by severity, keyword tables split into Priority and Other, and a Link Profile section. Configure Report saves on every change. On Saturday, AI Visibility arrived for discoveries and reporting: sampled mention rates for a client and its competitors across the major AI assistants, on location-pinned buyer prompts, reported as rates rather than single-run ranks.

Elsewhere: tasks got a page of their own with comments and inline editing, keyword mapping got a page-first view, planning stacks the soonest months on top, alerts read "All clear" when quiet, and a Help Center opened. Partners choose per client who implements website changes, their own team or ours. Google Business posts moved to a model where the plan is the written posts and the partner approves finished units. Citations became something the agent sets up and runs as recurring rounds.

## Behind the scenes

The migration from Workbench began on Wednesday: a partner-by-partner script that carries clients, keywords with rank history, tasks, notes, locations, Business Profiles, competitors, Google connections and past discoveries, then legacy agreements onto the new scope model. A runbook lets any session run a partner.

The agent's wiring became data. Wake bindings say why and when an agent runs (a critical alert, a finished gap review, a partner message, a schedule), each with its own skill stack and context, editable from an admin page. Skills became documents with a routing field. A Monday retrospective reviews shipped work on two horizons, a two-week pulse for harm and a month for verdicts, so the week's plan acts on findings the same morning. Agent Labs records every run as a replayable recipe and lets staff fix a skill side by side with the run that exposed the problem. A task run can now park itself and resume as the same session, so research, waiting for rankings and mapping happen in one head.

A shared Task Queue pools implementation work for specialists with atomic pick-up. A public API and MCP server opened for staff. Legacy partners got a second house brand, so a migrated agency sees the look they already know.
