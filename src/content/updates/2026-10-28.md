Most of today went into the partner API, and the rule behind it is worth saying plainly: the API reads the campaign and administers the workspace, and the SEO work stays with us. The rest of the day went to how a website gets started and how Listings talks about a business that is not a dental practice.

## For our partners

1. The partner API now covers the workspace: reports, your team and invitations, delivery settings, webhooks, report defaults, the WordPress plugin, brands and client contacts. Every one has a matching tool for partners who work through an assistant.
2. Website setup is an intake rather than a build. Three ways to start: pick an industry theme, have your current design rebuilt on the platform, or build it yourself.
3. Listings pages say "business", "team members" and "people's pages" unless the record really is a practice, and every row now says whether its link is dofollow.
4. On a Tidysite, a heading or paragraph is edited where it stands, a section can be shared across pages, and every section type can be seen in one place.

### 1. What the API opens

Reports are a first-class read: one client's filed months with download links, or the report for a month, quarter or span in the same shape the reporting page shows it, including the written summary and the true ad spend. A download link is good for a week.

Delivery settings read and change at all three layers (workspace, brand, client), including handing work to the client's own people. Webhooks list every event the workspace can send, the contract a receiver implements, and the signing secret. The team roster, invitations, report defaults and client contacts are all there, and inviting a client contact to their portal is one call.

Writes that create work, tasks and research sessions, stay with us. Reads a partner already has in the app (keywords, locations, competitors, citations, scope, connections) are open on both sides. The activity read now returns the Activity tab's feed, sentence by sentence, rather than raw event rows.

### 2. Three ways to start a website

The setup screens used to wait on us, and there was a staff door behind them for when they stalled. Now the first screen asks how you want to start and the site is provisioned the moment you choose, with a warning first, because building is a commitment.

Setup collects what the business is, where it works and what it sells. The service area is drawn on a map and saved as you close it, with the towns inside the shape found for you; a business its customers come to skips that screen entirely. Build-it-yourself ends on a connect screen with the address and the command to point your own tooling at it. A rebuild of your current design ends on a confirmation and opens a thread with your account manager. A site that was never published can be deleted so you can start over.

### 3. A business is a business

The citation work grew up on dental practices, so the screens said "the practice", "practitioners" and "doctor pages" to a roofer and a home care company. Each location now reads as a practice or a business from its own industry and categories, and the wording follows, including stored notes and the client hand-off file. Nothing to set, and it is right the moment a record changes.

The link column reads dofollow, nofollow or a dash when the page has not been read yet, and it sorts. The location filter on the hand-offs page fits the screen now, and side panels across the app open at the width they were meant to.

## Behind the scenes

An agent classified a client as an online retailer when the client runs a wellness clinic. It had searched the business name, found a same-named clothing brand and answered from the snippet without ever opening the site. We replayed it and got the same answer every time, which told us it was the instruction, not luck. The agent that reads a business and decides what kind of business it is now opens the site and reads the home, services and about pages before it answers, and it has been told not to trust a same-named company. The other small reading agents moved to a sharper default at the same time.

The support team now sees broken Google connections where they work, before a partner writes in. One row per client lists what is wrong (a profile with no working connection, an analytics or search console link whose account broke, a property gone from the account), with the posts that are failing or coming due beside it, and one button opens a conversation about all of it. A row leaves the list when the thing heals, so the list can be worked to zero. Our own demo workspaces no longer clutter it.

Our reference for the API is now generated from the same registry that enforces it, so what is documented and what exists cannot drift. We extended the idea inward: every route our own workers and sweeps call carries a row saying why it exists, who calls it and when it runs, with a test that fails if a route has no row or a row has no route. One endpoint was renamed along the way so the public surface stops naming an agent.

Two directories rebuilt their sign-up forms this month and our claim steps were rewritten against them, including waiting out a save that takes a minute and reading the result back. Those recipes now also explain how their category maps were built, which is what the next person needs when a new kind of business shows up.
