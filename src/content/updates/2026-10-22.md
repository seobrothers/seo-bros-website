A citations day. The work that cannot be automated now has somewhere to sit, and the checks that decide whether a listing is right got considerably less literal.

## For our partners

1. A Listing hand-offs page sits in the sidebar under "For your review": every listing step waiting on a person, grouped by who owes it, longest waiting first.
2. The checks behind a listing's score now read an address written another way, and a profile named for one of the practice's dentists, as right rather than wrong.
3. The website page shows a build tracker: five stages from staging site to ready for you, each with its state and one line in plain words.
4. When an approval is handed to a client, their contacts are emailed as the request opens, at the same moment the partner notice goes out.

### 1. The hand-off board

Each item shows the site, the step in full, how long it has been waiting, and a button that opens the listing. Steps waiting on the practice carry a Mark done button that records the step exactly as the listing sheet does. The same strip now sits above the table on a location's Listings page, so you can see what one location owes without leaving it. A claim someone has since finished by hand drops off on its own, so the board shows only what is actually left.

### 2. Judging a listing

A listing used to read wrong when a directory wrote the same address another way: a unit stuck on the house number, "FM 685" for "Farm to Market 685", the town repeated inside the street line. Those read as matches now. Profiles that put the dentist's name in the listing name, surname first and trailing letters included, read as the practice's. And a page showing only a town with no house number reads as missing rather than a mismatch, which is the difference between "add this" and "someone else is in your spot". Stored listings pick up the new reading on their next check.

## Behind the scenes

Deciding what counts as a missing field took a survey rather than an opinion. We went through every client listing we have read and asked, site by site, whether any of them ever shows a given field. A site only joins the never-shows-it list when none of its listings carries the field and a live page has no link out to the business's own site. Two sites looked like candidates and were left out: the field is there on their pages and our reader was missing it. That is a fix for the reader, not a rule for the site.

The agent that claims directory pages also looks before it signs up now. Before creating a page on Nextdoor it hunts for one that already exists, by the addresses the site builds from a business name and by search, then hands over the claim instead of making a second page. We added it after a pre-check found that a good share of the locations we were about to work had a page the audit had not caught, where creating another one either fails quietly or leaves a duplicate behind.
