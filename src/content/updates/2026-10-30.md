Today went in two directions: what you can read and do over the API, and the tooling behind rebuilding a client's old website so it matches the original.

## For our partners

1. The API reference opens from your avatar menu on its own page, in your workspace's colours, with its sections grouped the way you work.
2. A location's name, address and phone reach the listings checks wherever you change them, and the phone and name lists sit on the Locations page.
3. Your websites can sit on a domain of your own rather than a shared default, and on a staging site Sign in to edit and Request a change now work.
4. The tools behind rebuilding a client's old site can measure a page, capture it at four widths, and test its speed against the original.
5. Drafts save with full links only, and copying a draft out of the app keeps every link exactly as written.
6. Rank tracking carries on without a gap when a keyword's tracking entry is rebuilt at the source.

### 1. API documentation in the workspace

Sections run in the order a reader works (Account, Clients, SEO Service, Website Service), the sidebar scrolls the whole way down, and the text speaks to you about your clients and your packages. Two doors joined it: you can read a task's conversation threaded and reply to it, with internal notes staying internal, and a location can be added by Google place id, which fills in name, address, phone and website and attaches the Business Profile.

### 2. Locations and listings

A location's record and the copy that listing audits and corrections are checked against used to be two separate things, so a correction made on the Locations page never reached what we audited. They are one now: the Edit sheet, each Use Google's click and the API all write both. The other numbers and names a location uses, and the ones that are out of date, sit under each record, with the tracking flag so a call-tracking number is not reported as an error.

### 4. Rebuilding an old site

Matching the original is most of the work in a port. The tools now report what a page actually renders at (type sizes, weights, spacing, colours, boxes) instead of leaving it to be guessed from a picture, capture a page at phone, tablet, laptop and wide widths, open menus and dropdowns the page keeps closed at rest, and run a speed test on the new page and the old one in the same call. Pictures pushed in are resized and re-encoded on the way, so a hero image does not land at four megabytes. Publishing a website that is live on a client's own domain now takes a person's confirmation; an assistant working alone cannot do it.

## Behind the scenes

Most of today went into the machinery that runs the servers client sites sit on. We can now add a server and a site, take nightly backups into our own storage, restore one with a copy of what was there taken first, move a site to another server with the exact records it needs spelled out, manage its databases and extra domains, and hear within minutes when a server stops answering from several places at once. Much of it was writing down steps for tooling we had already built for other parts of the fleet.

We also changed what finishing means. Shipping a change now records where to check it and how, and asks the person who raised it to confirm. The ticket waits in sign-off until they answer; Not done sends it back with what is missing. Alongside that, a row in the queue can be claimed so two people do not spend the same ten minutes on the same thing, and a line marks what arrived since you last looked.

Two smaller pieces of hygiene: the build now refuses a change to the data behind the app while the running version still depends on it, and data syncing stops for clients who have left instead of retrying against doors that are closed.
